Assessment rules

Course: Artificial Intelligence: Social, Cultural and Legal Challenges (monographic lecture) Academic year: 2026/2027, winter semester Lecturer: Dr Karol Kasprowicz

These rules follow from version 2 of the syllabus.

1. Form of assessment

Graded credit. The student chooses one of three paths (A: test, B: application project, C: own proposal) and informs the lecturer of the choice by 6 November 2026 (meeting II), in class or by e-mail. If no choice is notified, the test is deemed to have been chosen.

Attendance at the lecture is not a condition for obtaining credit.

2. Path A: written test

Element Number Points
Single-choice questions 20 1 point each
Open questions (a case or a problem to assess) 2 5 points each
Total 30 points
  • Time: 45 minutes.
  • The closed questions cover all 10 topics, two questions from each. Test questions are not published. The quizzes on the course website cover the same material, but their questions do not appear in the test.
  • The open questions concern two different meetings. They test the application of legal provisions to a short set of facts and the critical assessment of a problem.
  • No materials, telephones or AI tools may be used during the test.

3. Path B: course project

The project is a concept for an AI-based application that helps lawyers with a specific task, together with a plan for its implementation. No programming is required: we assess the idea, its legal justification and the feasibility of its implementation. A working prototype (e.g. built with a no-code tool) is welcome but not required.

  • Topic: an application from the list of sample ideas or one of the student’s own, agreed with the lecturer by 6 November 2026. The submission consists of one sentence: who the application is for and which task it helps with.
  • What the project must contain:
    1. Problem and user: a specific legal task (e.g. verifying case reference numbers in a pleading, reviewing contracts for unfair terms), who performs it today, how much time it takes and where errors are made.
    2. Application concept: functions, the user’s workflow step by step, the role of AI (what kind of model or technique, e.g. a language model with retrieval from a database of legislation and case law), input data and sources, the point at which human oversight takes place. In addition, a diagram of how the application works and a sketch of the most important screens.
    3. Legal analysis: classification under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act) (the AI Act) (whether it is an AI system, the risk category, obligations, including Articles 4 and 50), personal data protection (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR)), professional secrecy, rights to data and sources, liability for errors made by the application, rules of professional ethics.
    4. Risks and safeguards: hallucinations and how results are verified, bias, data security, misuse; what the application does when it does not know the answer.
    5. Implementation plan: stages (from a pilot in one law firm, court or law clinic to wider use), timetable, required resources and approximate costs, user training, measures of success, method of monitoring and the conditions under which implementation should be suspended.
  • Form: a PDF document of 4–5 pages (approximately 7,000–9,000 characters including spaces) excluding appendices, with footnotes and a bibliography. Appendices: a diagram of how the application works, a sketch of the screens, and possibly a link to a prototype.
  • Submission deadline: during the examination session, no later than 18 February 2027 (the last day of the session), by e-mail to the lecturer’s address.
  • Assessment criteria: application concept and its usefulness to a lawyer 30%, legal and ethical analysis 30%, implementation plan, risks and human oversight 20%, sources, structure and writing 20%. Detailed descriptions of the levels are given in the document “Project assessment criteria”.
  • AI tools: their use is permitted, including when building a prototype. A declaration on the extent of AI use must be attached to the project (template on the course website). The student is responsible for the accuracy of all sources, legal provisions and judgments cited.

4. Path C: the student’s own proposal

The student may propose their own form of assessed work related to the subject matter of the course, e.g. a working prototype of an AI tool for lawyers, an analysis of a judgment or a case with a legal assessment, a popular-science article, an educational podcast or film, a report on a small questionnaire survey, or participation in a project of a law clinic or a student research group.

  • Submission: by 6 November 2026, by e-mail. A short description (up to half a page) is sufficient: what will be produced and in what form, which legal or social problem related to AI it concerns, which sources the student will use and how the student proposes the work should be assessed.
  • Approval: the lecturer accepts the proposal, asks for changes or rejects it. If the proposal is rejected, the student chooses Path A or B within one week of the reply.
  • Scope: comparable to a Path B project. Each piece of work is accompanied by a short written commentary (1–2 pages) setting out the legal basis and sources.
  • Assessment criteria: set by the lecturer when approving the proposal; they always include legal correctness and the use of sources. The same grading scale applies as for the other paths.
  • Submission deadline: during the examination session, no later than 18 February 2027, by e-mail (a file or a link to the work).
  • AI tools: permitted, with a declaration on the extent of AI use (template on the course website).

The test point thresholds have been rounded up to whole points.

6. Participation

Participation may raise the final grade by half a grade, provided that the grade is a pass. The following count:

  • taking part in discussion in class;
  • completing the tasks with generative models from the “Exercises and discussion” section on the course website and briefly discussing them in class;
  • the quizzes on the course website (the “Test yourself” section in each district).

Quiz results are stored only in the student’s browser and are not sent anywhere. The best result for each district is shown on the “Score card” on the course home page; the card can be shown to the lecturer or printed.

Project assessment criteria

The project is a concept for an AI-based application that helps lawyers with a specific task, together with an implementation plan. It is assessed on a 100-point scale. The final grade is determined according to the scale in the assessment rules: 60 points is the pass threshold.

Assessment sheet

Criterion Weight Very good Good Satisfactory Unsatisfactory
Application concept and its usefulness 30 points 27–30: a specific task and user, a real problem described with data or an example; a well-thought-out workflow, an appropriately chosen AI technique, a clearly identified point of human oversight; a clear diagram and sketch of the screens 23–26: a good idea with minor gaps in the description of how it works or of the data 18–22: a general idea (“a lawyer’s assistant”), with little shown of what specifically it helps with 0–17: no specific task, or the application makes no technical sense
Legal and ethical analysis 30 points 27–30: correct classification under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act) (the AI Act) (AI system, risk category, obligations of the provider and the deployer); Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR), professional secrecy, liability and professional ethics applied to this application rather than described in general terms 23–26: a correct analysis with minor gaps 18–22: provisions listed but poorly related to the application 0–17: incorrect classification, or the AI Act or the GDPR omitted
Implementation plan, risks and human oversight 20 points 18–20: realistic stages including a pilot, resources and costs, training, measurable criteria of success; risks (hallucinations, bias, data security) with specific safeguards and conditions for suspending implementation 15–17: a correct plan with isolated gaps 12–14: a vague plan, risks merely listed 0–11: no plan or no risks
Sources, structure and writing 20 points 18–20: legislation, case law, literature and data used critically and correctly cited; logical structure; precise language 15–17: minor flaws in citation or writing 12–14: few sources, numerous flaws 0–11: no sources, or sources that are unverifiable or non-existent

Formal requirements

  • Title page: first name and surname, student ID number (numer albumu), name of the application and a one-sentence description (for whom, for which task), date.
  • Structure following the five parts set out in the assessment rules: problem and user, concept, legal analysis, risks, implementation plan.
  • Appendices: a diagram of how the application works and a sketch of the most important screens (hand drawing, mock-up in any tool); a prototype is optional.
  • Footnotes in the style accepted in legal scholarship; a bibliography divided into legislation, case law and literature.
  • For online sources, the date of access.
  • Declaration on the use of AI tools (template: “Declaration on the use of AI”).
  • PDF file, named Surname_Firstname_projekt_AI.pdf.

Typical mistakes that lower the grade

  • “A lawyer’s assistant for everything” instead of an application for one specific task.
  • Assuming that the model makes no mistakes: no way of verifying the results and no response to “I don’t know”.
  • Sending client data or case files to a public chatbot without an analysis of the GDPR and professional secrecy.
  • Omitting classification under the AI Act, especially where the application supports a court or an authority (Annex III, point 8), and omitting the dates of application of the provisions following the amendments made by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 [title to be verified].
  • An implementation plan without a pilot, costs and criteria of success.
  • Citing judgments without a case reference number and date; sources generated by AI and not checked.

Project topics

This applies to Path B (Path C is the student’s own proposal; the rules are in the assessment rules). The list is indicative. The project is a concept for an AI-based application that helps lawyers with a specific task, together with an implementation plan (rules in the “Path B” section). Students may propose their own idea and agree it with the lecturer by 6 November 2026. For each idea, the course topics that will be most useful are given in brackets.

Law firm and work with documents

  1. Citation checker for pleadings. Checks whether the judgments, case reference numbers and legal provisions cited exist and say what the pleading attributes to them (SAOS, CBOSA, ISAP). (topics 3 and 8)
  2. Consumer contract review. Identifies terms similar to clauses held to be unfair and proposes changes for the lawyer to assess. (topics 5 and 8)
  3. Due diligence assistant. Organises a company’s documents from a virtual data room and draws up a list of risks to be checked by the team. (topics 3 and 4)
  4. Procedural deadline calendar. Reads served documents and proposes deadlines with their legal basis; the lawyer approves each entry. (topics 3 and 8)
  5. Legal change monitor for a law firm. Tracks the Dziennik Ustaw (Journal of Laws), EUR-Lex and case law in selected fields and summarises changes for clients. (topics 2 and 9)

Courts, authorities and public administration

  1. Anonymisation of judgments before publication. Detects personal data in judgments and proposes replacements; a court official approves the result. (topic 8)
  2. Support for a court referendary in order-for-payment cases. Checks the completeness of the statement of claim and documents in simple payment cases. Requires particular analysis of Annex III, point 8 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act) (the AI Act). (topics 7 and 8)
  3. Municipal legal information chatbot. Answers residents’ questions about local taxes and refers difficult matters to an official. (topics 1 and 7)
  4. Official letters in plain language. Translates decisions and summonses into understandable language without changing their legal effects. (topics 6 and 7)
  5. Assistant for responding to requests for access to public information. Classifies requests and identifies deadlines and grounds for refusal for the official to assess. (topic 7)

Data protection, compliance and the AI Act

  1. AI system classifier under the AI Act. Guides a compliance department through a series of questions and indicates the likely risk category, with reasons and a list of obligations. (topic 7)
  2. GDPR assistant for a small business. Helps to maintain a record of processing activities and to prepare a data protection impact assessment (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR)). (topic 8)
  3. Tool for assessing deepfakes in evidence. Supports a legal representative in assessing whether a recording or photograph may have been generated, and in preparing an application for the taking of evidence. (topics 5 and 8)
  4. Sustainability reporting assistant. Collects data on the environmental footprint of a company’s IT services for the purposes of reporting and due diligence. (topic 10)

Access to law and education

  1. Student law clinic: case intake. Organises the client’s description of the case, identifies missing information and the area of law; the student and the supervisor take the case forward. (topics 4 and 8)
  2. Assistant for people in a dispute with a bank or carrier. Explains consumer rights and helps to prepare a complaint, with a clear boundary between legal information and legal advice. (topics 5 and 8)
  3. Case trainer for law students. Generates cases in a chosen field and assesses solutions according to the lecturer’s criteria. (topics 4 and 6)
  4. Legal terminology translator. Supports a sworn translator in choosing equivalent terms across two legal systems, with references to sources. (topics 3 and 6)

Each idea must be narrowed down: identify one user (e.g. a legal adviser (radca prawny) in a small law firm, a court referendary, a municipal official) and one task in which the application genuinely shortens the work or reduces the risk of error.
